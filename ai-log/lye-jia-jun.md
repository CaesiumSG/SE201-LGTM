## Reflection

### What I learned about using these tools well
I learned that a clear spec up front matters most. When I set up the
repository, I gave the AI our project structure, the tech stack my team had
agreed on, our timeline, our limits and our goals, and I had it work
milestone by milestone. Two things surprised me. I found that a model given
tools won't always use them. I also saw for myself that a cheap local model
can write vulnerable code: when I asked one for a new feature, without
hard-coding anything risky myself, it introduced a real vulnerability that
our own agent then flagged. I also found that working in worktrees and
through pull requests let me run several pieces of work in parallel with
few conflicts.

### Where the tools were strong, and where they were weak
I realised the tools were strongest at building front ends and demos
quickly, and at running tests and evaluations for me. I also realised they
were weak whenever I didn't give them a proper spec: they hallucinated or
assumed things I never intended. I learned to align the AI with what I
actually wanted before letting it start.

### What went wrong, and what I would do differently
What I struggled with most was speed. On larger repositories our agent was
slow and my GPU cluster jobs kept timing out, and I couldn't get faster
GPUs. In the end I added a cloud API backend, which also let me compare
cloud models with our cluster model, and I found that comparison
interesting in itself. I also learned that whether I write code myself or
with an AI agent, and even after I review it, I can still miss things. So
I would keep my teammates in the loop from the start, with clear
responsibilities, so that someone else checks my work.
